Selling in Folsom

Most prep advice is a list of forty things.

These are the seven that actually move your number, what staging really does, and what to skip so you can launch sooner and keep the money.

$70,000

That is the spread between the highest and lowest sale on one Broadstone floor plan in the last year. Four identical houses, same builder, same street pattern. They closed between $700,000 and $770,000.

Square footage did not move that. Condition, presentation and the launch price did. Which is the whole argument for spending a little on the right things and nothing at all on the wrong ones.

Closed prices from Sacramento County public records, four bed, three bath Elliott Homes plan, 1,834 to 1,851 square feet.

What pays, in order

Buyers decide twice. Once in about thirty seconds of scrolling photos, and again in about thirty seconds of standing in your entry. Money spent where those two moments happen comes back. Money spent anywhere else mostly does not.

Light

Every bulb working and all the same color. Mismatched bulbs are the most common thing we fix and the easiest to see in a photo. Open every blind, pull back every curtain, turn on every lamp the morning of the shoot. This is the cheapest item on the list and it shows up in all thirty photos.

Under $100

Emptying, not cleaning

Clear the counters down to two things. Take half of everything out of the closets and the pantry and the garage. A half empty closet reads as generous. A full one reads as too small, even when it is the same closet. Rent a storage unit for a month if you need to. It is the best money in the whole plan.

$100 to $300

The front, from the street

Your first photo is the front of the house and it decides whether anyone sees photo two. Mow, edge, fresh mulch, wash the front door and the house numbers, and buy a new mat. If one shrub is dead, pull it. You do not need to landscape. You need the photo to look cared for.

$150 to $600

Paint, but only where it is beat

A full interior repaint rarely pays on a house that has been kept up, and it costs you two weeks. Scuffed hallways, door frames, and baseboards do pay. So does one accent wall in a color nobody else would have picked. Walk the house with fresh eyes, or have Joe walk it, and paint only what a stranger would notice.

Varies, get two bids

Floors in the rooms people stand in

If the carpet is stained in the living room or the primary bedroom, replace it there. Buyers forgive a worn bedroom upstairs. They do not forgive the room they are standing in while they decide. Tired hardwood usually just needs a professional clean, not a refinish.

Room by room

Smell

You cannot smell your own house. Nobody can. Ask someone who does not live there to walk in and tell you the truth, and believe them. Pets, cooking and damp are the three, and all three are fixable in a week. This is the single most common thing that kills a showing and the single thing sellers never hear about.

A week and a favor

A pre-inspection

Around $500 to have an inspector walk it before a buyer does. You find out what they are going to find out, you fix the small things on your own timeline instead of under a deadline, and you have a report to hand people at the open house. On an older house it is the best five hundred dollars in this entire document, because it kills the repair renegotiation before it starts.

About $500

What to skip

Every one of these gets suggested to sellers constantly. On most Folsom houses they cost more than they return, and several of them delay your launch.

A kitchen or bath remodel right before listingYou rarely get it back at this price point, and you lose four to eight weeks of market. If the kitchen is original, price it as original and let the buyer pick their own finishes.
New appliances, unless one is brokenBuyers do not pay a premium for a range they did not choose. Replace what does not work. Leave the rest.
Buying furniture to fill empty roomsThat is what staging is for, and staging is rented. Never buy a sofa for a house you are leaving.
Whole house repaint on a well kept homeSpot work costs a fraction and photographs the same. Repaint when the house genuinely needs it, not by default.
Landscaping projectsNew sod and a redesigned yard almost never return their cost. Mowed, edged and tidy does the job in a photograph.
Fixing things nobody can seeIf you would not have done it while living there, talk to Joe before you do it to sell. Some of it matters for inspection. Most of it does not move the price.

Staging, plainly

Staging is rented furniture and accessories, placed by someone who does this for a living, to show scale and flow. It is not decorating and it is not about taste. It is about a buyer walking in and understanding instantly how big the room is and what it is for.

When it earns its money

  • The house is empty. Empty rooms photograph smaller and feel cold.
  • The furniture is oversized for the room, which is the most common problem we see.
  • The house has been a rental and the pieces in it are whatever was left.
  • The floor plan is unusual and needs to be explained without words.

When to skip it

  • You live there, the house is furnished well, and it just needs editing and light.
  • Only one room is the problem. Stage that room, not the house.
  • The budget is tight and there is a pre-inspection or a carpet still unpaid for. Both of those come first.

There is no single right price for staging. It moves with the size of the house, how many rooms get done and how long you need it. Joe will get you two quotes before you commit to anything, so you are choosing from real numbers instead of a guess.

How the three weeks actually go

This is the order we run it. Yours may compress or stretch, but the sequence does not change, because photos have to come last and the listing cannot go public before it is filed with MetroList.

Week one, day one
Joe walks the house with you, room by room, and you leave with a short list and nothing on it that does not pay.
Week one
Book the pre-inspection. Order the preliminary title report and open escrow now, not after you have an offer.
Week one to two
The work gets done. Emptying first, because everything else is easier in an empty room.
Week two
Stagers come in if you are staging. Joe walks it again the same day and the final price gets set on what the house actually looks like.
Week three, morning
Photographer. Stills, a twilight exterior, a floor plan and a 3D walkthrough, all in one visit.
Week three
Photos back, listing filed with MetroList, and then everything fires on one day. Sign, cards, email, social, open house.
